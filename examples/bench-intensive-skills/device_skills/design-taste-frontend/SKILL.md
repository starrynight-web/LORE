Concise, distinctive landing pages and portfolios that avoid templated AI aesthetics. Reads the design brief first to infer the right aesthetic direction (SaaS minimalist, agency experimental, premium consumer, etc.) before touching code, preventing default AI patterns Configures three core dials—design variance, motion intensity, and visual density—that drive layout, animation, and spacing decisions throughout the page Enforces strict anti-patterns: bans em-dashes, generic serif fonts, beige+brass premium palettes, three-equal-feature-cards, and other LLM tells; audits every visible string for AI hallucination Implements real design systems (Material, Carbon, Fluent, Polaris, Primer, GOV.UK) when applicable; otherwise builds with Tailwind, Motion, and GSAP for custom aesthetics Requires a mandatory pre-flight checklist covering contrast, button wrapping, hero fit, color consistency, motion justification, and 50+ other production-readiness gates before shipping tasteskill v2 A page built by a model usually looks like the average of every page the model has seen: a centred headline over three lines, a little label above it, a row of icon cards, a soft gradient, a few thin rules and a monospace caption. Nothing in it is wrong and nothing in it was decided. This skill replaces those defaults with decisions, in four ways: A script deals the directions, you choose among them. Asked to invent a look, a model picks the category default every time. scripts/draw.mjs deals three of twenty researched directions; you choose the one that carries the subject's idea, and the script then deals its type system, palette, hero and section compositions from a library distilled from 247 real sites. The idea comes from the subject. In the study, what separated the best sites from the forgettable ones was a concept taken from the thing itself, not the styling. A house style with no ornaments, measured in a real browser by scripts/inspect.mjs, so the rules are checked rather than hoped for. You look at what you made. Screenshots at three widths, a juror's rubric, fix, look again.
